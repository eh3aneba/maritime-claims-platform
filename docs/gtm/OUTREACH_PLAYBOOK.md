# Founder Outreach Playbook v1.0

## Positioning
Do not lead with “AI for marine insurance.” Lead with the expensive workflow:

> H&M machinery claim files arrive as emails, PDFs, logs, workshops reports and invoices. MCRI turns that evidence into a source-linked chronology, missing-document workflow, technical/financial review and a human-approved initial assessment.

The key proof point is auditability: AI proposes; claims professionals approve.

## Warm introduction message
Hi [Name] — I’m building a marine-claims workspace focused initially on H&M machinery cases. It structures claim documents, identifies missing evidence, builds a source-linked chronology and supports technical/financial review without automating coverage decisions. I’m looking for a small number of claims professionals to test a controlled machinery-claim workflow and tell me where it fails. Would a 30-minute discovery conversation be reasonable?

## Cold email
Subject: H&M machinery claims workflow — design-partner discussion

Dear [Name],

I’m developing a specialist claims platform for H&M machinery losses. The current MVP takes documents such as Chief Engineer reports, Engine Logs, PMS/Running Hours, workshop reports, quotations and invoices and turns them into human-reviewed structured evidence, chronology, missing-document actions and an initial assessment.

It is deliberately decision-support only: causation, coverage and settlement remain with the claims professional.

I am inviting a small first cohort of marine claims teams to test the workflow on a controlled/anonymized machinery case and measure whether it reduces review time without weakening auditability.

Would you be open to a short discovery discussion to see whether this matches your current claims process?

Kind regards,
Ehsan

## LinkedIn connection / DM
I’m building a source-linked H&M machinery claims workspace and looking for a few marine claims teams to stress-test the workflow. It focuses on evidence review, chronology, missing documents and cost control—not automated coverage decisions. I’d value a short practitioner discussion if relevant to your work.

## Follow-up cadence
- Day 0: warm intro or email
- Day 4–5: concise follow-up with one concrete workflow example
- Day 10–12: final low-pressure follow-up
- Stop active follow-up after three unanswered touches; keep the account in nurture rather than generating spam.

## Discovery call goal
Do not demo in the first five minutes. First establish:
1. machinery claim volume and severity;
2. how files arrive and are reviewed today;
3. time spent on first file review / chronology / missing evidence / cost schedule;
4. what causes rework or leakage;
5. who uses the workflow;
6. who approves a pilot / software purchase;
7. data and security restrictions.

Only demo the parts tied to pains the respondent actually described.

## Commitment ladder
Discovery → controlled demo → measured design-partner pilot → commercial interview → paid pilot proposal.
