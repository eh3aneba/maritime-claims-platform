# Design Partner Commercial Interview Script v1.0

Use this after the participant has completed the product task. Do not ask all questions mechanically; capture evidence in the Pilot Console.

## 1. Current process and cost
- How many machinery/H&M claims does your team handle in a typical year?
- Who touches a claim before the first assessment is issued?
- How much senior-review time is normally required?
- Which part of the workflow creates the most delay or rework?
- If the product saved the time observed today, where would that capacity be used?

Record annual relevant claim volume, expected users, and a reasonable fully-loaded hourly cost if the participant can provide one. Do not invent missing assumptions.

## 2. Value hierarchy
Ask the participant to rank what matters most:
- faster first review;
- fewer missing documents;
- stronger audit trail;
- chronology/conflict detection;
- technical consistency;
- cost-control/quotation review;
- management visibility;
- private deployment / confidentiality.

Ask: **Which one of these would make you actively push this internally?**

## 3. Buyer and champion
- Who owns the problem operationally?
- Who can approve a paid pilot?
- Who owns the annual software budget?
- Who would block this for security or IT reasons?
- Who needs to be in the next meeting?

Capture roles, not personal names unless there is a legitimate business need to store them.

## 4. Buying process
- Where would this purchase sit: claims budget, IT, innovation, operations, or another budget?
- Does a pilot require procurement or legal review?
- What spend threshold changes the approval process?
- How long would a normal purchase take?
- What security documentation is mandatory?
- Is cloud acceptable, or is private cloud/on-premise required?

## 5. Willingness to pay — ask in this order
### Unprompted
- If this worked on your real claim flow, what would a paid next step look like?
- Would you expect a paid pilot, annual platform fee, per-user pricing, or another structure?
- What budget range would be plausible enough to continue internally?

### Then test price anchors — internal experiment only
Do not describe these as established list prices. Test reaction to a paid pilot around **USD 5k / 10k / 20k** and an annual platform around **USD 20k / 40k / 75k**, adjusted for customer size and scope.

Ask:
- At which level is it an easy decision?
- At which level does it require a business case?
- At which level would you reject it?
- What additional value would justify the higher band?

Record the participant's language and range, not only the final number.

## 6. Objections and no-go conditions
- What would stop you from using this on a real claim?
- Which missing integration is actually blocking versus merely convenient?
- What would your security team object to first?
- Which AI behavior would make you reject the product?
- What evidence would you need before trusting this beyond a pilot?

## 7. Commitment question
End with a concrete commitment question:

**Based on what you saw today, what is the next action you are prepared to take, who owns it, and by when?**

Weak answer: "Send me information."
Strong answer: "Send a USD 10k paid-pilot proposal; I will review it with the Head of Claims next Thursday."

## Interview discipline
- Do not defend the product during objections.
- Do not convert feature requests into commitments.
- Do not count praise as WTP.
- Keep unknown fields unknown.
- Separate user preference from buying authority.
